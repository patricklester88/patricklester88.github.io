🎯 Why Location Can Be Inaccurate

Why your phone, computer or browser may sometimes show the wrong place.

Why is my location sometimes wrong?

Your device may combine GPS satellites, Wi-Fi networks, mobile phone masts and internet information to estimate your position. These sources can be incomplete, delayed or inconsistent, so the result may not always match your exact location.

🏙 Tall buildings

Buildings can block or reflect satellite signals, causing the reported position to shift.

🏠 Indoor use

Walls and roofs weaken GPS signals, so your device may rely more heavily on Wi-Fi or network positioning.

🌲 Trees and hills

Dense foliage, steep valleys and other obstructions can reduce satellite visibility.

📶 Wi-Fi databases

If a router has moved or has been recorded incorrectly, Wi-Fi positioning may place you in the wrong area.

📡 Mobile networks

Location based on nearby phone masts is usually less precise than a strong GPS result.

💻 VPNs and IP addresses

IP-based location may show your internet provider or VPN endpoint rather than your physical position.

📱 Device hardware

Older devices and laptops without dedicated GPS hardware may provide broader estimates.

🛰 Too few satellites

When only a small number of satellites are visible, the device has less information to work with.

How can I improve the result?

  • Move outdoors or near a window.
  • Allow a few seconds for the device to refine its position.
  • Enable Wi-Fi as well as location services.
  • Avoid tunnels, basements and areas surrounded by tall buildings.
  • Keep your browser and operating system updated.
  • Use a smartphone when you need the best chance of a precise result.

How WhereAmI reports location

WhereAmI displays the location information supplied by your browser and device. It does not independently calculate your position or control the quality of the GPS, Wi-Fi or network signals.

The displayed accuracy is an estimate and should not be treated as guaranteed legal proof of location.
