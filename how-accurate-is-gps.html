How accurate is GPS?

Modern GPS technology is remarkably accurate, but the result you receive depends on your surroundings, your device and the signals available at the time. Outdoors, many modern smartphones can estimate your location within a few metres. Indoors or in built-up areas, the estimate may be much less precise.

Typical GPS accuracy

EnvironmentTypical accuracy
Open countrysideOften within 1–5 metres
Parks and open spacesUsually within 3–10 metres
Residential streetsTypically within 5–15 metres
City centresOften 10–30 metres or more
Inside buildingsCan vary from tens to hundreds of metres

These figures are approximate examples. Actual accuracy depends on your equipment and local conditions.

What affects GPS accuracy?

🛰 Satellite visibility

The more satellites your device can receive clearly, the better the result is likely to be.

🏙 Buildings

Tall buildings can block or reflect signals, making the reported position less accurate.

🌲 Trees

Dense foliage can weaken satellite signals.

📱 Device quality

Newer phones often contain better GNSS receivers and antennas than older devices.

🏠 Indoors

Walls and roofs reduce satellite reception, so Wi-Fi or network positioning may be used instead.

🌐 Assisted positioning

Many devices combine GPS with Wi-Fi and mobile networks to improve speed and reliability.

Does more decimal places mean greater accuracy?

Not necessarily. A coordinate shown to six decimal places may look extremely precise, but the underlying measurement may still only be accurate to several metres. The number of digits displayed should not be confused with the true accuracy of the location measurement.

How can I improve GPS accuracy?

  • Go outdoors where there is a clear view of the sky.
  • Allow your device a few extra seconds to refine its position.
  • Enable Wi-Fi as well as GPS if appropriate.
  • Keep your operating system and browser updated.
  • Avoid standing close to very tall buildings where possible.
  • Use a modern smartphone with location services enabled.

How WhereAmI reports accuracy

WhereAmI displays the location estimate supplied by your browser or device. The reported accuracy is generated by your device's positioning system and is presented to help you understand how reliable the current estimate may be.

WhereAmI does not calculate the GPS accuracy itself and cannot improve or reduce the accuracy reported by your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GPS always accurate to a few metres?

No. Accuracy varies depending on the environment and the quality of available signals.

Why is my laptop less accurate than my phone?

Many laptops rely mainly on Wi-Fi positioning, while smartphones often include dedicated satellite navigation hardware.

Can weather affect GPS?

Normal weather has only a small effect, but local conditions and obstructions often have a much larger influence.
