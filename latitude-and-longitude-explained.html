Latitude and Longitude Explained

Latitude and longitude are the global coordinate system used to describe locations on Earth. Together, they can identify a place with far greater precision than a town name, postcode or street address.

What are geographic coordinates?

Geographic coordinates are numbers that describe a position on the Earth's surface. The two main values are latitude and longitude.

When combined, they create a unique coordinate for almost any location in the world.

51.5074, -0.1278

This example points to central London.

↕ Latitude

Latitude measures how far north or south a place is from the Equator.

It ranges from 90° north to 90° south.

↔ Longitude

Longitude measures how far east or west a place is from the Prime Meridian.

It ranges from 180° east to 180° west.

Understanding latitude

The Equator is the starting line for latitude and is labelled 0°.

  • Locations north of the Equator have positive latitude values.
  • Locations south of the Equator have negative latitude values.
  • The North Pole is approximately 90° latitude.
  • The South Pole is approximately -90° latitude.

Examples:

London: 51.5074


Sydney: -33.8688

Understanding longitude

The Prime Meridian is the starting line for longitude and is labelled 0°. It passes through Greenwich in London.

  • Locations east of the Prime Meridian usually have positive longitude values.
  • Locations west of the Prime Meridian usually have negative longitude values.
  • Longitude reaches up to 180° east or west.

Examples:

London: -0.1278


Tokyo: 139.6917

How coordinates are written

Format Example Description
Decimal Degrees 51.5074, -0.1278 The most common format used by websites, apps and GPS devices.
Degrees, Minutes and Seconds 51°30'26.6"N, 0°07'40.1"W A traditional format often used in navigation and mapping.
Degrees and Decimal Minutes 51°30.443'N, 0°07.668'W A mixed format used in some marine and aviation systems.

Which number comes first?

Latitude is normally written first, followed by longitude.

Latitude, Longitude

For example:

51.5074, -0.1278

This means 51.5074 degrees north and 0.1278 degrees west.

Some mapping systems may display longitude first. Always check the format before entering coordinates.

Why are coordinates useful?

📍 Precise locations

Coordinates can identify a place even when there is no formal address.

🥾 Outdoor activities

Walkers, hikers and explorers use coordinates to describe remote positions.

🚑 Emergency services

Coordinates can help communicate a location when street information is unclear.

📷 Photography and wildlife

Coordinates can record where an image, sighting or observation was made.

How accurate are coordinates?

The number of decimal places affects the apparent precision of a coordinate.

Decimal places Approximate precision
1 About 11 kilometres
2 About 1.1 kilometres
3 About 110 metres
4 About 11 metres
5 About 1 metre

More decimal places do not guarantee that the original measurement was truly that accurate. Device and signal quality still matter.

Frequently asked questions

Can two places have the same coordinates?

No. A latitude and longitude pair identifies one position on the Earth's surface, although rounded coordinates can cover a wider area.

Why are some coordinates negative?

Negative latitude values indicate locations south of the Equator. Negative longitude values indicate locations west of the Prime Meridian.

Can I paste coordinates into a map?

Yes. Most online maps accept decimal coordinates entered as latitude followed by longitude.

Are coordinates better than an address?

They can be more precise, especially in rural areas, parks, beaches, woodland and places without a street address.
