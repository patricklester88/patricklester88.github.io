Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about location accuracy, browser permissions, privacy, maps and printable WhereAmI certificates.

Using WhereAmI

Practical answers about how the location tool works.

Is WhereAmI free to use?

Yes. The location tool and educational GPS guides are free to use in a supported web browser.

How do I find my current location?

Open the homepage, press Find My Location, and allow location access when your browser asks.

Is the location exact?

No location system is perfect. Accuracy depends on your device, surroundings, GPS reception, Wi-Fi networks and browser settings.

Why is my phone usually more accurate than my computer?

Many smartphones contain dedicated GPS or GNSS hardware. Computers often rely more on Wi-Fi or IP-based estimates.

Why is the street or address sometimes wrong?

Coordinates are converted into an address using mapping data. The nearest mapped address may not exactly match your physical position.

Can WhereAmI work indoors?

Yes, but accuracy may be lower because walls and roofs weaken satellite signals.

Does it work outside the United Kingdom?

Yes, provided your device and browser support location services and you grant permission.

Why does the result take time to appear?

Your device may need several seconds to combine GPS, Wi-Fi and network information.

Privacy and permissions

You remain in control of location access.

Does WhereAmI store my location?

WhereAmI is designed to display the location supplied by your browser for the feature you request. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Why does my browser ask for permission?

Browsers require websites to obtain your consent before accessing location data.

What happens if I block location access?

The site will not receive your location and location-based features will not work until permission is granted.

Can I remove location permission later?

Yes. Major browsers let you change or remove permissions for individual websites.

Location certificates

What the printable result does—and does not—prove.

Can I print or save a certificate?

Yes. Use the print option and choose Save as PDF if available in your browser.

What information appears on the certificate?

It may include latitude, longitude, reported accuracy, date, time, approximate address and a map link.

Can I use it as legal proof?

No. It is a general informational record and should not be treated as legal, medical, emergency or safety-critical proof.

Why does it show an accuracy range?

The figure is an estimate supplied by your device and indicates the approximate radius of uncertainty.
