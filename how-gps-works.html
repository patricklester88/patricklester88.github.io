How GPS Works

GPS helps your device estimate where you are by listening to signals from satellites high above the Earth. This guide explains the process in simple language, why accuracy can vary, and how WhereAmI uses the location information your browser provides.

What does GPS mean?

GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It is a satellite navigation system used around the world by phones, cars, watches, cameras, aircraft, ships and many other devices.

GPS satellites continuously transmit radio signals containing timing and position information. Your device receives these signals and uses them to estimate your location.

🛰 Satellites

Satellites orbit the Earth and send precise timing signals.

📱 Your device

Your phone or computer listens for available satellite and network signals.

📍 Your position

The device calculates your approximate latitude, longitude and accuracy.

How does your device find its position?

GPS uses a method called trilateration. Instead of measuring angles, your device estimates how far it is from several satellites. When it has enough distance measurements, it can calculate where those distances overlap.

1️⃣One satellite gives a broad possible area.
2️⃣Two satellites narrow the possible area.
3️⃣Three or more satellites refine the position.
4️⃣A fourth signal helps correct timing errors.

A fourth satellite is normally needed because your phone's clock is not as accurate as the atomic clocks used by satellites. The extra signal helps correct very small timing differences.

GPS is not the only location method

Modern devices often combine GPS with other location sources. This is why your phone may find your location quickly even indoors, where satellite signals can be weaker.

  • GPS/GNSS satellites for outdoor positioning.
  • Wi-Fi positioning using nearby wireless networks.
  • Mobile phone towers to estimate a wider area.
  • IP address location, which is usually much less precise.
  • Bluetooth beacons in some indoor environments.

How accurate is GPS?

Accuracy varies depending on your device, surroundings and signal quality. Outdoors with a clear sky, GPS can often be very accurate. Indoors or near tall buildings, the result may be less precise.

EnvironmentTypical accuracy
Open outdoor areaOften within a few metres
Residential streetsUsually good, but may vary
City centre with tall buildingsMay be less accurate due to reflections
IndoorsOften relies more on Wi-Fi or network location
Laptop without GPS hardwareMay rely mainly on Wi-Fi or IP location

WhereAmI displays the accuracy estimate supplied by your browser or device. It should be treated as a useful guide, not a legal guarantee.

Why can GPS be inaccurate?

🏙 Tall buildings

Buildings can block or reflect satellite signals, especially in city streets.

🌲 Trees and hills

Dense tree cover, hills and valleys can weaken or obstruct signals.

🏠 Indoor use

Walls and roofs can block satellite signals, so your device may use Wi-Fi instead.

📱 Device differences

Phones, tablets and laptops can have different location hardware and settings.

How WhereAmI uses your location

When you press Find my location, your browser asks whether you want to share your location. If you allow it, the browser sends WhereAmI an estimated position.

WhereAmI can then show details such as your approximate address, latitude, longitude, accuracy estimate, date, time, map link and downloadable certificate.

WhereAmI does not control the satellite system or the calculations performed by your device. It presents the location information provided by your browser in a clear, useful format.

Frequently asked questions

Does GPS need the internet?

GPS signals come from satellites, but internet access can help your device load maps, speed up location detection and improve address lookup.

Why is my phone more accurate than my laptop?

Many phones contain dedicated GPS/GNSS hardware. Some laptops rely mainly on Wi-Fi or IP-based location.

Does WhereAmI track me?

Your browser controls location permission. WhereAmI only receives location information when you choose to allow it for the feature you are using.
